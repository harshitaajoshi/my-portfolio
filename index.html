About Me

Where I study and what I care about.

I’m a B.S. candidate in Computer Science and Mathematics at Texas Tech University’s Honors College, graduating in Spring 2028. I hold a Presidential Merit Scholarship and carry a 3.9 GPA, with coursework in data structures and algorithms, object-oriented programming, operating systems, and software engineering.

Most of what I enjoy sits at the point where a program meets the machine: query engines, memory layout, graph search, and real-time rendering. I learn these by shipping into real codebases rather than reading about them, which is why most of my work lives in public repositories and merged pull requests.

Outside of coursework I want to keep building developer tools — especially tools that make a large, unfamiliar codebase legible to the person who just joined it.

CodeSkyline

3D codebase visualization & bug prediction · Go, React Three Fiber, Cloudflare

A platform that turns any GitHub repository into a time-traveling 3D city of its own architecture. It predicts bug-prone files from churn × complexity at 3.5× the baseline rate (70% vs. 21%), backtested across 12 major repositories, and ships a GitHub Action that posts automated pull request risk reports flagging hotspots and suggested reviewers. Optimizing the WebGL renderer with GPU instancing and shadow caching took the Linux kernel view (97K buildings) from 11 to 50 FPS.

AICorp

Autonomous on-chain AI corporation · Solidity, Next.js, Node.js · Built at ETHDenver

An autonomous AI corporation in which four agents sell paid reports, earn USDC payroll, and pay for their own compute — all enforced on-chain. A Solidity treasury on Base Sepolia logs revenue, payroll, and compute spend while enforcing solvency across 139+ transactions, with x402 HTTP micropayments and 0G decentralized inference feeding a live dashboard of real-time profit and loss.
